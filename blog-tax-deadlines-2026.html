2026 CRA Tax Filing Deadlines for Individuals & Businesses in Ontario

By TORUL Tax Advisors  ·  Mississauga, Ontario  ·  January 2026

Missing a CRA deadline doesn't just mean a late-filing penalty — interest starts accumulating immediately on any balance owing. Whether you're an employee, self-employed, or running a corporation in Ontario, here are the key dates you need to know for the 2026 tax year.

Quick note: These dates apply to the 2025 tax year return filed in 2026. When a deadline falls on a weekend or holiday, CRA typically moves it to the next business day.

Individuals (T1 Personal Tax Return)

Deadline Date Who it applies to
File your T1 return April 30, 2026 Most individuals
Pay any balance owing April 30, 2026 All individuals — interest starts May 1
RRSP contribution deadline March 2, 2026 To deduct on your 2025 return
TFSA contribution room resets January 1, 2026 All TFSA holders

Self-Employed Individuals

If you or your spouse/common-law partner carried on a business in 2025, your filing deadline is extended — but your payment deadline is not.

Deadline Date Notes
File your T1 return June 15, 2026 Extended deadline
Pay any balance owing April 30, 2026 Still April 30 — don't be fooled
Quarterly instalment payments Mar 15 · Jun 15 · Sep 15 · Dec 15 If CRA has asked you to pay instalments

💡 Common mistake: Many self-employed people assume the June 15 filing extension also delays their payment. It doesn't. Any balance owing still accrues interest from May 1, 2026. File by June 15, but pay by April 30.

Canadian-Controlled Private Corporations (CCPCs)

Deadline Timing Notes
File T2 corporate return 6 months after fiscal year-end e.g., Dec 31 year-end → June 30 filing deadline
Pay balance owing (eligible CCPC) 3 months after fiscal year-end If previous year tax < $3,000
Pay balance owing (other) 2 months after fiscal year-end Most corporations
Monthly instalment payments Last day of each month Based on prior year taxes

What are instalment payments?

If your corporation owes more than $3,000 in federal tax, CRA generally requires monthly instalment payments rather than one lump sum. Failing to pay instalments on time results in instalment interest — even if you pay the full amount at year-end.

GST/HST Deadlines

If you're registered for GST/HST in Ontario, your filing frequency determines your deadlines. Most small businesses file annually; higher-revenue businesses file quarterly or monthly.

Filing frequency Typical threshold Filing & payment due
Annual filer Taxable revenues ≤ $1.5M 3 months after fiscal year-end
Quarterly filer Revenues $1.5M – $6M 1 month after quarter-end
Monthly filer Revenues > $6M 1 month after month-end
Not sure if you need to register? If your total taxable revenues exceed $30,000 in any single calendar quarter or over four consecutive quarters, CRA requires you to register for GST/HST. If you're near that threshold, it's worth planning ahead.

Late Filing Penalties — What They Cost You

The CRA late-filing penalty is 5% of the balance owing, plus 1% for each full month late, up to a maximum of 12 months. If you've been penalized before, those rates can double.

On top of penalties, CRA charges daily compound interest at the prescribed rate on any unpaid balance from the day after the payment deadline.

Best practice: Even if you can't pay in full, file on time. Filing on time stops the late-filing penalty from applying — you'll still owe interest on the balance, but you avoid the penalty on top of it.

Final Checklist Before April 30

Here's a quick pre-deadline checklist for individual filers:

  • Gather all T4 slips (employment income)
  • Collect T5 slips (investment income), T3s (trust income)
  • Confirm RRSP contributions were made before March 2
  • Track any business/self-employment income and expenses
  • Note any home office, vehicle, or medical expenses for deductions
  • Make sure your direct deposit info is on file with CRA (My Account)

When in doubt, a quick consultation with a qualified accountant can save you far more than it costs — especially if you have self-employment income, rental properties, or a small corporation.
